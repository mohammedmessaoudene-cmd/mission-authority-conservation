# A possible later documentary study

**Candidate design only. Not preregistered, not accepted by a partner and not executed.**

The first feasibility description commits nobody to this design. Its purpose is to avoid recruiting someone under an understated promise of effort or an assumed result.

## Study question

For one actual non-production incident, does an additional narrative organization of the evidence help a reader reach a justified understanding with acceptable effort, compared with the integrator's strongest existing records and workflow?

This is a study of documentary work on an incident that already happened. It neither induces another failure nor replays the operation. If only a synthetic example is available, it remains a synthetic example and is not substituted for a real integration case.

## A comparison worth attempting

The integrator would first confirm the relevant existing tools and runbook. Both presentations would retain the same original records, provider documentation, available observations, ability to ask questions and permissions. The comparison must not weaken the existing solution or give the additional note exclusive information. Readers may always consult the same original evidence.

The precise note and all materials must be authorized for this study before distribution. This public brief does not supply a private structured format, its grammar or a replacement verifier. Studying a newly written narrative note would not establish the performance of an exact P1 implementation or artifact.

One possible exploratory design uses two readers, one per presentation. Each sees the evidence available when confirmation was missing, then the evidence available later. The later material must not overwrite the earlier answers. The case owner keeps the reference evidence separate and an assessor checks what each answer actually supports.

Randomly assigning two readers cannot isolate a format's causal effect from differences between those readers. A reader seeing both presentations of the same learned case also introduces carryover. Results would therefore be descriptive observations of this case, not a causal estimate, general error rate or proof of equivalence.

Selection metadata, filenames, titles and instructions must not reveal a later resolution to a reader judging the earlier evidence. If that information is already known, record the limitation and do not describe the earlier judgment as blinded. Training and clarification must be equally available; no private corpus is required or distributed.

## What would be recorded

Before opening the case to readers, agree on the exact question, reference evidence, coding rules, materials, roles, budgets, inclusion criteria and stopping rules. Date and retain the agreed version. A local hash detects changes; it is not independent registration or proof that a case was previously unknown.

Record separately:

- Unsupported conclusions about an effect, proposed retries unsupported by available evidence, and claims extending beyond what an observation establishes.
- Information available in the source but lost in the reader's account, distinguished from information absent from both presentations.
- Unnecessary informational holds, distinguished from justified uncertainty or a lack of current authority to act.
- Active reading, preparation, training, note construction and correction time; waiting time and unmeasured work are reported separately.
- Missing answers, disagreements and non-adjudicable conclusions. None is silently counted as zero errors.

Use raw counts with denominators and actual minutes. Two chronological views of one incident are dependent observations, not two independent incidents. A single case does not justify a population error percentage or a statistical significance claim.

## Work and stopping

A two-reader design could require a data custodian, two readers and an assessor. An internal planning ceiling is **420 person-minutes (7 person-hours)**: 120 minutes preparing admissible evidence, up to 105 minutes per reader including training, and 90 minutes for assessment and restitution. Coordination and any difficult redaction can add effort and must be estimated explicitly before agreement. This is not a promise of a seven-hour total or a demand made by the first invitation.

If only one person or an initial 20–30 minute discussion is feasible, call it a non-comparative feasibility review and do not calculate an integration gain from it. No such discussion is scheduled or agreed by this document.

Stop if safe evidence, willingness, time, roles, authorization or a credible reference are unavailable; if additional operational access or effects would be needed; or if the comparison becomes asymmetric. Preserve the reason rather than inventing substitute observations.

The study can conclude that the extra note adds no value or costs more effort. A favorable observation would be a local signal for a separately scoped follow-up, not grounds to announce a new standard. Publication of participant data or attribution would require a separate agreement; discussing a case is not permission to publish its evidence.
